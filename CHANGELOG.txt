----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Version: Unreleased
Date: 11.10.2026

  Changes: 
    - Updated the game engine to Bevy 0.19.
    - New save layout: one folder per world, with world.ron for the metadata, one file per chunk and one file per player. Worlds from older versions are converted on first load, the old file is kept as world.ron.legacy.
    - The world and the connected players are saved automatically every 60 seconds.
    - Block changes are now sent to the players as small updates instead of whole chunks.
    - World generation is deterministic: a seed always gives the same world.
    - Added a chat (T key).
    - Added sounds: footsteps and landings, mining, breaking and placing blocks by material, other players' blocks and steps heard from where they are, and button clicks. Their volumes are set in Settings > Audio.
    - Fixed players spawning inside stone.
    - Fixed the world not being saved when leaving the game.
    - Fixed the fox sinking into the ground; it now stays on it.
    - Animals live in the world: herds of sheep on the plains and mountains, foxes in the forests and on the ice plains. They wander, sheep graze, and foxes flee the players.
    - Added fox and sheep spawn eggs: used on a block, they bring their animal there.
    - Added cows, pigs, chickens, rabbits, wolves, horses, goats and cats, each with its biomes and its spawn egg. The fox has a new voxel model, in the style of the other animals.
    - Animals collide with the players, who push them by walking into them, and with one another.
    - Fixed crashes when a client sent invalid data: usernames are checked (1 to 32 characters) and the number of inputs per tick is limited.
    - Fixed the constant position corrections (rollbacks) of the player: the client now resumes its prediction from the full state sent by the server.
    - Fixed the server list being emptied when its file could not be read.
    - Fixed a world disappearing from the list although its deletion had failed.
    - Fixed players being announced at the position of the last player who joined.
    - Fixed crashes when a texture pack lacks a texture, when a port is already in use and when the connection cannot be opened: the game logs the error and goes back to the menu.
    - Fixed blocks breaking faster at higher frame rates: a block now takes the same time to break on any screen.
    - The mob draw distance can no longer go below one chunk.
    - Added a render distance, from 1 to 64 chunks (12 by default), set in Settings > Video or with O and P: the server sends the chunks within it around each player and unloads the farther ones. A dedicated server caps it with --max-render-distance (64 by default).
    - Fixed every player receiving the chunks around all the other players.
    - A broken block now drops its item on the ground as a small spinning copy, which the players pick up by walking over it. Items of the same kind lying together merge, items left for 5 minutes vanish, and the items on the ground are saved with the world.
    - Added the chest, crafted from a ring of eight planks: right click opens it, and its 27 slots keep their items in the world, saved with it. A broken chest drops what it held. Sneak to place a block against a chest or a crafting table instead of opening it.
    - Added sprinting (Left Ctrl while walking forward, also in flight), sneaking (Left Shift: slower, and a sneaking player never walks off an edge) and swimming: sprinting in the water swims where the player looks, Space and Left Shift swim up and down.
    - A jump is now 1.3 blocks high, instead of 6.8.
    - Fixed holding jump under a block, which stuck the player under it.
    - Added the survival and creative game modes, set with /gamemode <creative|survival>. New players start in survival: they have 10 hearts, cannot fly, and lose one point of health per block of a fall beyond 3. Health comes back by a point every 4 seconds. A dead player sees a death screen, and Respawn takes it back to the spawn of the world.
    - Added wooden tools, crafted on a crafting table from planks and sticks like in Minecraft: the pickaxe, the axe, the shovel, the hoe and the sword. A tool breaks the blocks it is made for twice as fast (stone for the pickaxe, wood for the axe, dirt and sand for the shovel, leaves for the hoe and the sword), and wears out after 59 blocks; its slot shows a bar of what is left. Stone, cobblestone and furnaces now drop nothing unless broken with a pickaxe.
    - Added coal, iron and gold ore. Like stone, they drop something only when broken with a pickaxe: coal ore drops coal, and iron and gold ore drop themselves, to be smelted.
    - Biomes are about three times wider, with winding borders, and the land slopes into the sea instead of stepping at the border of two biomes. Shores are sand; the seas are deeper, and their uneven floors are sand in the shallows, then gravel and stone deeper down. Worlds created before keep their explored land; new land around it uses the new generation.
    - Added mountain ranges: long chains of peaks up to about 165 blocks high, bare stone above 104 and snow-capped above 122, with steep faces left bare. The mountain biomes now come from the altitude.
    - Added rivers that wind through the lowlands in sandy beds and valleys, and lakes with gently sloping shores that deepen toward their middle, on uneven beds of sand, gravel and stone. Water in the ice plains freezes over.
    - Added caves: wide winding tunnels that run level or sloping and open at the surface through grass, sand and snow, and wide chambers deep underground. They stay clear of the water of the seas, rivers and lakes.
    - Added gravel, found on the floors of the seas, lakes and rivers.
    - Ores generate in clusters and veins: coal up to y 128, iron up to y 64, and the rarer gold up to y 32.
    - Added the jungle, along the warm and wet coasts: jungle trees, bushes, ferns and tall grass, growing denser from its border to its heart, and in its heart giant trees, with a trunk two blocks wide, that rise high above the rest, vines hanging from them and from the jungle trees, and cocoa pods on some jungle trees, under their crown. Added jungle logs, planks and leaves, with the recipes of their wood, the fern, the vine, which hangs on the side of the block it is placed against, and the cocoa beans that a broken pod drops.
    - The furnace works: right click opens it, with an input slot, a fuel slot under a flame and an output slot behind an arrow. With fuel (coal, wood, wooden tools, sticks) it smelts sand into glass and cobblestone into stone, an item every 10 seconds, also while nobody watches, as long as its chunk is loaded; lit, it glows. Shift-click sends what smelts to the input and what burns to the fuel. A furnace faces the player who placed it, keeps what it holds in the save and drops it when broken.
    - Added progression: stone, iron, golden and diamond pickaxes, axes, shovels, hoes and swords, crafted in the shapes of the wooden tools from cobblestone, iron ingots, gold ingots and diamonds. Each tier mines faster and lasts longer, as in Minecraft (gold is the fastest and wears out the soonest), and harvests harder blocks: iron ore needs a stone pickaxe, gold and diamond ore an iron one, or they break without dropping anything. Diamond ore lies deep, 5 to 16 blocks above the bottom of the world. The furnace smelts iron and gold ore into ingots, diamond ore into diamonds, and logs into charcoal, which burns like coal and makes torches. Nine ingots or diamonds make a block of iron, gold or diamond, and a block gives them back. The item browser shows the smelting of an item beside its crafting recipes, and how many items a fuel smelts.
    - Added the bucket, of three iron ingots in a V: right click takes a water source into it, and right click with the water bucket pours the source elsewhere, against the aimed face, where it flows. A bucket filled from a stack goes into the inventory, or is dropped when there is no room for it.
    - Fixed the hearts covering the bottom row of the open inventory in survival: they hide while it is open.
    - Crafting takes the gestures of Minecraft: holding the left button with a stack on the cursor and moving over cells splits it evenly between them, the counts showing in each cell before the release, and the right button drops one item in each cell it crosses.
    - Shift+click on the result of the crafting grid crafts as many times as the ingredients and the room in the inventory allow, into the inventory.
    - A number key 1 to 9 over the result crafts it once into that hotbar slot.
    - A double click on a stack gathers the stacks of its item onto the cursor, the smallest first, up to a full stack.
    - A Shift+double click on a slot moves every stack of its item to the other side: into the open chest or furnace, or between the backpack and the hotbar.
    - The inventory is heard: a soft tick when a stack is picked up, a tap when it is put down, a knock of wood for a craft and a dull thud for a refused action, whose slot shakes with a red border.
    - A recipe made of planks takes any planks, the species mixed in the grid as in Minecraft: the item browser shows one card for it, its planks cycling, and filling the grid takes a single species whenever one is enough.
    - Added the Drop key, Q by default: in game, Q throws one item of the selected hotbar slot and Ctrl+Q its stack; inventory open, Q throws from the slot under the cursor, and a click outside the panel throws the stack held (right click: one item). A thrown item flies a couple of blocks ahead and cannot be picked up for 2 seconds.
    - The item browser is a faster recipe book: Ctrl+click on an item fills the crafting grid with its first recipe that can be made now (Ctrl+Shift+click: as many times as possible); + Fill grid on a recipe missing ingredients lays its ghost over the grid, faint, the lacking cells in red; a Recent row under the bookmarks holds the last 9 items crafted.
    - Added the inventory tweaks of the Mouse Tweaks mod, on by default and switched off in Settings > Controls: Shift+drag moves every stack the cursor crosses, and the mouse wheel over a stack moves one item of it where a Shift+click would send the stack.
    - Added building blocks: hewn stone, limestone and slate (pockets in the stone), clay (in the shallows), fired bricks and brick walls, rammed earth, thatch, blocks of coal, the lantern, and wooden, hewn stone and brick slabs. The spruce leaves are an item.
    - Added copper and tin, smelted and alloyed into bronze, whose tools stand between stone and iron.
    - Added willows by the water, acacias in the savanna, reeds, heather and mushrooms, fiber and rope, and flint, sometimes found in gravel. A mushroom roasted in a furnace is eaten with a right click and gives a heart back.
    - Added stairs, doors, ladders, fences, glass panes and hanging lanterns. Players step up onto slabs and stairs without jumping.
    - Saplings, sometimes dropped by leaves, grow into trees.
    - Animals can be hit: sheep give mutton and wool, which is also taken with a right click and grows back as they graze. Added the spear, which reaches farther.
    - Added berries, tilled soil (with a hoe), rye, which the rain grows faster, rye bread, and the bedroll, which sets where a player comes back and skips the night once everyone sleeps.
    - Added the barrel, the rain cistern, which the rain fills and buckets empty, the campfire, which cooks and which the rain puts out, and the sign, written with /sign <text> and read with a right click.
    - Added the weather: clear spells of 10 to 100 minutes, then rain or, one time in three, a thunderstorm of 5 to 15 minutes, the same for every player and saved with the world; /weather <clear|rain|thunder> sets it. Rain rolls in over 20 seconds under thicker, darker clouds and a grey sky that hides the sun, dims the light and closes the fog in. Drops slanted by the wind splash on the ground and ring the water, sheets of rain fill the distance, and roofs, leaves and caves shelter from it; snow falls in the ice plains and above the snow line, nothing in the deserts and the savannas. In the High and Ultra shaders the ground open to the rain darkens and glistens, puddles gather in its hollows and mirror the sky, and it all dries after the rain. Thunderstorms strike lightning from the clouds, a jagged bolt every player sees alike, whose flash lights the world, the sky and the clouds, and whose thunder cracks or rumbles after it, as late as sound travels. The rain hisses in the open, drums on a roof and is muffled under water, at its own Weather volume in Settings > Audio.
    - Reworked the biomes: they are 2.5 times larger, with frayed, irregular borders instead of round blobs, and are chosen from five climate parameters (temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion, weirdness) that also shape the coasts, the hills and the mountain ranges. Rivers cross the land. New biomes: Ice Spikes, rare in the cold, Flower Forest, Grove and Forest Clearing in patches inside the plains and forests, and Jungle Edge along the jungles. Worlds made before keep their chunks: the new terrain meets them at a seam.
    - Added world types, chosen in the New world dialog: Default, Large biomes and Archipelago; a world of one biome is made by writing generation: (preset: SingleBiome("desert")) in its world.ron.
    - Added /locate biome <biome>, which tells the nearest place of a biome.
    - New players spawn deep inside a temperate biome, in a clearing, instead of on the land nearest the centre of the world.
    - Biomes are registered by code, for the mods to come: a server sends its biomes to the players who join, and the game warns when they include biomes it does not know.
    - Survival can kill in many ways: falls, drowning (10 bubbles of air over the hotbar, then 1 heart a second), fire, lava, starvation and the hostile mobs. A hit leaves the player invulnerable for half a second, reddens the edges of the screen and plays a hurt sound, and the death screen tells what killed the player.
    - A player who dies in survival drops its whole inventory, the stack under its cursor and its crafting grid included, scattered where it died; the items lie there for 5 minutes. Creative keeps everything. Respawning, at the bedroll or the spawn, brings back full health, food and air.
    - Added fire, lit with a flint and steel: it spreads to wood, leaves, grass, wool and thatch, burns them away, and goes out in the rain. A player in it burns for 4 seconds, which water and rain put out, and sees flames at the bottom of the screen.
    - Added lava: it flows slowly over 3 blocks, lights what burns around it, fills the bottom of the caves, sets a player in it burning and hurts it fast, and hardens into obsidian or cobblestone where water meets it. The bucket takes and pours it, and a lava bucket is the longest furnace fuel.
    - Added hunger: 10 drumsticks over the hotbar that sprinting, swimming, jumping, mining, fighting and healing empty. Health only comes back from 9 drumsticks up, a player with 3 or fewer cannot sprint, and an empty bar starves. Food fills the drumsticks instead of healing: raw and roasted pork, beef, chicken, rabbit and mutton, leather and feathers from the animals, carrots, potatoes and baked potatoes, grown on tilled soil, which water within 4 blocks grows twice as fast.
    - Blows knock mobs back and wear the weapon, a hit mob flashes red and is heard, and every animal drops its loot.
    - Added seven hostile mobs, which spawn in the dark around the players and despawn far from them: Gnawers, rats in packs in the caves; Brambles, bushes that bite in the woods at night; Rollers, boulders that roll at you in the caves and on the peaks; Stumplings, walking stumps of the night grass that sink back at dawn and fear fire; Drowners, which drag swimmers down in deep water; Cinderlings, which hover in the deep caves and throw burning embers, and go out in water and rain; Gust Owls, which dive at the players of the peaks at night and throw them off the ledges. Each has its spawn egg. Sneaking halves any knock back. /difficulty peaceful removes them and keeps them away, /difficulty normal brings them back.
    - Added villages to new worlds: a square around a well, gravel streets lit by lanterns, houses, forges, granaries, fields of rye and pens of animals, built of the wood and earth of their biome (plains and forests, savanna, desert, ice plains). Their chests and barrels hold food, ingots and tools. /locate village tells the nearest one. Worlds created before keep their land as it is.
    - Players have a body: a head, a torso, arms and legs cut from a skin, which walk and run, crouch, swim lying flat, strike with the right arm and turn the head where the player looks. Seen in third person and by the other players.
    - Added skins: twelve characters ship with the game, chosen on the new Skin screen of the main menu; a model wearing the chosen one slowly turns there and on the main menu, over the Skin button. Drop a 64x64 PNG (or an old 64x32 one) on the window to add your own, kept in the skins folder of the game folder. The other players see your skin.
    - Every creature has a voice of its own: animals cry now and then, hostiles are heard approaching and striking, and each kind has its own hurt. Original music plays now and then, different by day, by night, underground and in the menus, with a Music volume in Settings > Audio.
    - Animals breed: feed two of a kind their food (rye for cows, sheep, goats and chickens, carrots for pigs, rabbits and horses, potatoes for pigs too, berries for foxes) and they meet and have a young one, which grows up in 10 minutes, sooner when fed. Wolves and cats are tamed with meat, follow their owner, and breed once tamed. Hearts rise over an animal that wants a mate.
    - The player now sees its arm and the item it holds in the lower right of the screen: it bobs while walking, swings when mining or hitting, pushes forward when a block is placed, and changes when another hotbar slot is selected. The other players, and the player itself in third person, hold their item in the hand of their model.
    - Servers have operators, who alone run the commands that change the game (/gamemode, /give, /time, /weather, /difficulty, /locate), and /op, /deop, /kick, /ban, /pardon and an optional /whitelist; /list names the players online. A kicked, banned or refused player sees why. In singleplayer you are always an operator.
    - Added the mouse sensitivity to Settings > Controls, and the field of view and fullscreen to Settings > Video: each applies at once and is kept.
    - The New world dialog offers the game mode its players start in, survival unless creative is chosen, and the difficulty. Two difficulties are new, easy and hard: hostile mobs strike softer or harder. Worlds created before keep theirs.
    - Added armour: helmets, chestplates, leggings and boots of leather, bronze, iron and diamond, worn in four slots of the inventory. It absorbs a share of the blows of mobs, of fire and of lava (from 28% for a leather suit to 76% for a diamond one), wears out, shows in a bar above the hearts and on the players, and is dropped at death.
    - Added dungeons in new worlds: ruins on the surface with a cellar, crypts off the caves, and sanctuaries by the lava, with better loot the deeper, guarded by hostiles that keep coming unless a light stands near. /locate ruin, crypt or sanctuary finds the nearest one.
    - Added villagers in the villages not yet explored: farmers, smiths and cartographers, who walk about by day and go home at night. Right click one to trade: they buy what you gather for coins and sell food, tools, armour and the way to the nearest dungeon, their stock renewed every morning.
    - Enchantments: an engraving table spends levels of experience on the item put in it. Efficiency, durability, sharpness, protection and bow power, up to three levels each, for a price shown beforehand. Enchanted items are tinted and marked with a gem.
    - Animals and hostile mobs float instead of sinking: they keep their head out of the water, animals swim to the shore and hostiles toward their prey, and they climb onto banks a block high. Drowners still swim under water.
    - A new world starts in the morning, in full daylight, instead of at sunset.
    - Added the bow and arrows: hold the right button to draw, slower on foot, and release to shoot, harder the longer it was drawn. The arrow falls as it flies, hurts mobs and other players, sticks in blocks and can be picked up again. A bow takes five sticks and a rope, four arrows a flint, a stick and a feather; the dungeons hold some, and pieces of armour.
    - Added experience: hostile mobs killed by the players, coal and diamond ore, and what is taken out of furnaces give orbs, which fly to the players and fill the level bar over the hotbar. Each level costs more than the one before. A dead player loses its levels and leaves half of their points where it died, up to 400.
    - Underground, hostile mobs are harder, more numerous and more rewarding the deeper they appear: the caves, the depths below y = 20 and the furnace around the lava lakes, where they are twice as strong. A message names each the first time a player reaches it.
    - The Ember Mother, mother of the Cinderlings, sleeps in a huge cavern around a lake of lava at the bottom of the world, a few hundred blocks from the origin. A depths compass, of four gold ingots around a diamond, points to it. She wakes when a survival player comes in, throws embers and calls Cinderlings, and under half her health hunts the players and sets the ground on fire; she sleeps and heals once nobody is left. Beaten for good, she leaves the Ember Heart, a glowing trophy block, and plenty of experience, and the credits scroll. She never wakes in a peaceful world, and worlds from before the dungeons have no lair.
    - The main menu opens the credits, which also name where the textures, fonts and sounds come from.
    - Added 21 achievements, from the first tool to the Ember Mother, kept per player in each world: a toast tells each one, and Progress in the pause menu lists them all. None are earned in creative.
    - Added statistics of each player in a world, on the Progress screen and in the end credits: play time, greatest depth, blocks mined, creatures beaten and deaths.
    - Added hints for the first minutes of a new player in a survival world, from walking to placing torches, with the keys bound. Each shows once. Settings > Interface turns them off.
    - Added sounds to the later features, all synthesised for the game: armour put on and taken off (by material) and breaking, the bow drawn and loosed, arrows hitting blocks, creatures and players and picked up, experience orbs and levels, the engraving table, trades (coins) and villager voices per trade, hearts, births and tamings, mobs falling into water, and short stings for a new depth, an achievement, a boss, the victory and the credits. Other players' are heard where they are.

Date: 25.11.2024    

  Changes: 
    - Added biomes with 6 types: Plains, Forest, Medium Mountain, High Mountain, Desert, and Ice Plain. Biomes are determined using Perlin noise (temperature and humidity).

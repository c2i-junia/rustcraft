# Villagers and trade

Every village has villagers, and each one is a farmer, a smith or a cartographer. They buy what
you have plenty of for [[Coin|Coins]], and sell food, iron tools, armour, torches, and the way to
the nearest dungeon.

## How to trade

- Use a villager (right click) to open its offers.
- A trade takes what it costs from your inventory and gives you the goods. It is refused if you
  cannot pay.
- The trade window closes if you move more than **5 blocks** away.
- Your first trade earns the achievement [[page:achievements|A Fair Price]].

### Coins

Coins are the only money, and nothing crafts them. Earn them by selling to villagers, or find
them: shipwreck chests hold 2 to 9 half the time, buried treasure chests 8 to 24, and a fishing
treasure can be 4 coins. See [[guide:structures|structures]] and [[guide:fishing|fishing]].

### Stock and restock

Each offer has a stock: how many times a villager makes it in a day. Once it is used up, the
offer waits. Every villager restocks all its offers **at sunrise**. Stock is counted per
villager, so two smiths sell twice as many swords.

### Directions to a dungeon

The cartographer's last offer gives no item: it tells you in the chat where the nearest dungeon
is, of any kind (ruin, crypt or sanctuary), searched within 2048 blocks. The answer names the
kind, the distance, one of eight compass points, and for crypts and sanctuaries how many blocks
up or down. For example: *"The nearest dungeon is a crypt, 100 blocks to the north, 40 blocks
down"*.

In a world without dungeons the reply is *"No dungeon is known in this world"*. Read more about
dungeons in [[guide:structures|structures]].

## Villagers

- By day a villager walks about its home and rests between walks. **At night it goes home and
  stays there.** It walks through doors, and stands still while it trades.
- Nothing can hurt a villager: blows, arrows and splash potions leave it be. Hostiles ignore
  villagers and hunt only players.

### Professions

Each small or big house of a village gets one villager, born inside it. Professions go in turn
in the order the houses are built: farmer, smith, cartographer, then farmer again. A village with
three houses or more has all three. Other buildings, like the forge, the granary or the fields,
have no villager.
